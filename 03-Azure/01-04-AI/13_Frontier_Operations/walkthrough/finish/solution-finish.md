## What a good canvas looks like

A strong first canvas:

- names a process the team **knows**, with an owner who would sponsor a pilot;
- ticks three or more readiness boxes honestly, and says which step has no API;
- separates **instructions** (the outcome), the **skill** (the method) and **tools** (access);
- names who approves what, and which sites the agent may reach;
- estimates time and cost today against the agent, even roughly;
- defines a four-week pilot with one measurable result, for example *all supplier statements for one business unit reconciled within a day of month-end, with no more corrections than today*.

Processes teams often choose: supplier onboarding and due diligence, expense and invoice checks, insurance claims triage, contract renewals, compliance evidence collection, customer order exceptions, access reviews.

## A good first skill

`publish-skill` accepts it (no TODO, valid name, a description), and a new person could follow it without asking questions. It has exact numbers and named categories, and a **Safety** section. It contains no real names, customer data or secrets.

## Points to discuss

- **Start with the owner, not the technology.** The best first process is one where the owner feels the pain and can say what "good" looks like.
- **Measure before you scale.** Challenge 7's numbers are the template for the pilot's.
- **What not to automate.** Processes where the judgement *is* the job, or where the rules change daily, are poor first candidates.
