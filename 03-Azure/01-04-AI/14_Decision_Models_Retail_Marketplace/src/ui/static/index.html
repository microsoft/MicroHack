Zava Marketplace opens on Monday at 09:00

Sellers will send about 10,000 listings a day. Some will sell banned products, hide a phone number to dodge fees, invent features, or pick the wrong category. Trust & Safety has three reviewers.

Your job this weekend: build the Listing Gate — let honest listings go live in seconds, fix what can be fixed, send only the hard cases to a person, and never put a dangerous listing in front of a customer.

How you'll build it — one piece of the gate at a time

Each piece asks a few closed questions about every listing — yes or no, which one, how good — to a decision model, Microsoft-Decision-1.1. It answers each with a probability in about a quarter of a second, for a fraction of a cent. Your rules turn the answers into publish, fix, review or reject.

Safety check: keep banned products and hidden contact details out

You ran last week's listings through an empty gate. Everything went live in a second — including things that must never reach a customer.

The mysteryWhat went live with an empty gate?

InvestigateAsk a decision model about a listing

Open each listing, predict what the gate should do, then ask. Look at the probability next to every answer.

  • ZM-0072 — a weedkiller with no product number
  • ZM-0056 — a smart plug with a "quick deal"
  • ZM-0005 — an ordinary garden hose
  • Then compare with a chat model: in the listing panel, click Ask a chat model the same questions. What's missing from its answers?
Browse all 24 starter listings

TryAsk your own yes/no question

A decision model answers any closed question. Try "Is it meant to be used outdoors?" on the hose, then on the desk lamp. A probability near 0 is a confident no; unsure is near the middle.

BuildAdd the Safety check to your gate

Two yes/no questions — prohibited_item and off_platform_contact. Your rule: above 80% it's a yes and the listing is rejected; below 20% it's a no; anything in between goes to a person.

What you learned: a decision model answers a closed question with a probability, fast and cheap, and writes almost nothing. Your code turns that probability into an action — the model doesn't decide, your rules do. A chat model can answer too, but without a probability you can't tell a sure "no" from a guess.
Challenge 2 · Saturday

Truth check: does the listing tell the truth?

Banned products are out. Now Priya from Trust & Safety sends over her draft of the truth check: does the listing match the manufacturer's facts, show the right photo, ask a sensible price — and is there anything aimed at the reviewer rather than the shopper?

🎯 You build: the Truth check🧠 You learn: how to ask a question a model answers well

PredictPriya's first draft: one question for every claim

Her draft asks one choice question: is the listing consistent, contradicted, unsupported or not assessable compared with the manufacturer's facts? How often will it be right on the 24 starter listings?

Why it fails: "consistent" and "unsupported" can both be true of one listing, so the model splits one probability between them. The gate asks two yes/no questions instead — does anything contradict the facts? and does it claim something the facts don't mention? One idea per question.

BuildAdd the Truth check to your gate

Five questions — contradicts the facts, invented claim, photo shows a product, photo matches the title, text aimed at the gate — plus two checks in code: the product number is in Zava's catalogue, and the price is between 40% and 250% of the usual price.

The mysteryWhat still gets through?

Open ZM-0050 and read the description to the end. Ask the gate's questions: what does addressed_to_gate say?

FixReword the question that missed it

The question describes text that asks the gate to do something. The note doesn't ask — it claims a check already happened. Decision models read literally. Extend the question so it also covers notes claiming the listing was already checked, approved or verified. Keep it one idea. Save, then test it on the starter set.

All of the gate's questions

BuildCarry your fix into the rehearsal

Re-asks only the question you changed on the 1,040 rehearsal and Monday listings (2–3 minutes). Your scorecard then uses your answers.

TryWhy is the price check code, not a question?

Open ZM-0066, a desk lamp at €13.54, and ask "Is the price less than 40% of the usual price?". The model answers about 50% — a coin toss. Look in Show me the code: it was never told the usual price. A division is one line of code, always right and free. Keep exact maths in code.

What you learned: one idea per question; plain words; give it what it needs to know; keep exact checks in code; and test every change — on the starter set, then on a thousand listings.
Challenge 3 · Sunday morning

Shelf check: the right aisle, and good enough to sell

Safe and truthful isn't enough. A desk lamp filed under kitchen is invisible to shoppers, and a listing shouting "!!! LIMITED STOCK !!!" sells nothing.

🎯 You build: the Shelf check🧠 You learn: choice and score questions — and why every choice needs a way out

The mysteryLive — but in the wrong aisle, or not worth reading

InvestigateChoice and score questions

  • Choice — one answer from a list: ZM-0089 is a desk lamp listed under kitchen. Ask the gate's questions and look at category: a probability for every category. Code compares the winner with the seller's choice and fixes it.
  • Leave a way out: "Gift idea!" — ask for a category without "cannot tell", then again with it. Where did the probability go?
  • Score — how good, on a scale: ZM-0060 ("!!! LIMITED STOCK !!!") against ZM-0005. Levels go lowest first.

BuildAdd the Shelf check to your gate

A choice question for the category (with cannot tell): if the model is at least 70% sure and disagrees with the seller, the gate fixes the category. A score question for listing quality: if it's probably poor or unusable, a person looks.

Sneak peekA new product arrives on Monday

Sellers have announced a product the gate has never seen: a smart LED bulb (ZM-0828), listed under smart_home. Ask the gate's questions. Is the model sure about the category? Is a smart bulb a light, or a smart-home device? Remember the answer for Monday.

What you learned: a choice question picks one answer and shows how the probability spreads; a score question rates on a scale. Always give the model a way out — cannot tell, other — or it's forced into a wrong answer. And when two labels are both reasonable, that's a policy decision, not a model problem.
Challenge 4 · Sunday afternoon

Go-live dial: what's automatic, and what a person checks

The gate works. Now two people disagree in the launch meeting.

"Your gate sends … hours of reviews a day to three people. Cut it." — Marta, CFO
"Not one more dangerous listing in front of a customer." — Sam, Head of Trust & Safety
🎯 You build: the go-live dial and your launch plan🧠 You learn: the line between automatic and human is a business decision

TryTurn the dial by hand

Every yes/no question has two lines. Above yes the gate acts as if the answer is yes; below no as if it's no; between them, a person decides. Move them for one question and watch your scorecard.

no if the probability is at most0.20
yes if the probability is at least0.80

Try unsupported_claim: drag no up to 0.50, then 0.95. When does it start letting invented claims through?

BuildPrice the mistakes, and let the console set the dial

What does each mistake cost, in reviewer-minutes? Marta and Sam have to agree on these numbers — not the model. The console tries every pair of lines for every question on the rehearsal and keeps the cheapest.

SeeMonday 09:00 — the doors open

516 listings the gate has never seen, including products that didn't exist last week. You tuned on the rehearsal; this is the real test. Open it once — every opening is counted, because if you keep tuning until Monday looks good, Monday stops telling you anything.

TryBe the reviewer

Run your gate on the 24 starter listings. Everything it isn't sure about lands here. Open a listing, decide — publish, fix the category or send it back to the seller — then see whether that was the right call. What would make a reviewer faster?

What you learned: a probability becomes a decision only at a line you choose — and the right line depends on what each mistake costs, which is a business decision. Uncertain answers go to a person. Measure on listings you didn't tune on, and say how bad it could still be: 0 bad listings out of 400 isn't zero.
