## Present the talk

1. Get the talk, then open `deck/index.html` in Edge or Chrome. Everything is in the folder:
   no server, no install, no internet needed. To download only this hack instead of all of MicroHack:
   ```bash
   git clone --depth 1 --filter=blob:none --sparse https://github.com/microsoft/MicroHack.git
   cd MicroHack && git sparse-checkout set 03-Azure/01-04-AI/14_Decision_Models_Retail_Marketplace
   ```
   Or use **Code, Download ZIP** on GitHub and unzip it.
2. Press **S** for the speaker view (notes and timer), **F** for full screen, **Esc** for the overview.
3. Each slide builds by itself when you arrive. When a slide has more to show, it stops and a small
   **Click to continue** cue appears at the top right: press the right arrow (or click) when you are ready.
   The notes mark every click with **[click]**.
4. The controls at the top right of every built slide: **Pause** (and Resume), **Replay** (from the start
   of the slide) and **Show all** (the final state at once). Going back shows the previous state without
   replaying it.
5. A slide's own buttons hand it over to you: the column headings on slide 3, the cards on slide 6,
   the listing chips on slide 9, the two lines of the dial on slide 10 (drag them, or focus a handle and
   use the arrow keys), the measures on slide 12 and the examples on slide 15.
6. Slide 14, **Sellers strike back**, is the moment of the talk. Ask the room "anything wrong?", wait
   for answers, then click **Audit the outcome**, and later **Fix the question** (or press the right arrow).

With reduced motion turned on in the operating system, or with `?static` after `index.html`, every step
appears at once.

**PDF of the slides:** open `deck/index.html?print-pdf` in Edge or Chrome and print to PDF
(landscape, no margins, background graphics on). Every slide prints in its final state, without controls.

The deck uses Sitka, Segoe UI Variable and Cascadia Code, which come with Windows. On other systems it
falls back to Georgia, the system font and Consolas or the system monospace font.

## Where the numbers come from

[`deck/js/data.js`](deck/js/data.js) holds every number the slides animate, with a comment naming its
source. It is generated by [`deck/tools/extract_data.py`](deck/tools/extract_data.py); to regenerate it
after the recorded runs change, run from `src`: `python ../techtalk/deck/tools/extract_data.py`.
Nothing in the extractor calls a model.

- Slides 1, 4, 6, 9, 10 and 11 use the hack's recorded Microsoft-Decision-1.1 and gpt-6-luna runs
  (`src/data/recorded/decision-all.jsonl` and `llm-all.jsonl`), read with the hack's own gate library.
  On slide 4 the regular expression really runs in the browser; the two model answers race at their
  recorded latencies.
- Slide 3 uses the scorecards in the walkthroughs of Challenges 1 and 4, slide 12 the comparison in
  Challenge 6, and slide 14 the live runs in Challenge 5 with the rehearsal impact and question wording
  from Challenge 2.
- Slide 5 is the real call from the Build track (`first.py`).
- Slide 7 uses Microsoft's announcement,
  [Microsoft-Decision-1.1 is now generally available](https://commandline.microsoft.com/microsoft-decision-model-ga/)
  (17 November 2026), and the [Ignite 2026 Book of News](https://news.microsoft.com/events/ignite/2026/book-of-news/).
  These are Microsoft's figures, not measured in the hack.
- The story clock on the chapter slides (65 and 19 hours to doors open) follows the hack's timeline,
  Friday 16:00 to Monday 09:00. The banking and manufacturing examples on slide 15 are illustrative
  designs, not measured results.

The slides use [Reveal.js](https://revealjs.com) 6.0.2 (MIT licence, in `deck/vendor/`).
