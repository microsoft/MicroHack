Chapter 3 Take it home

Same pattern, any small decision

A probability must never quietly become authority.

[19:30, 1.5 minutes] I talk across the stages as they light up one by one.

Let's zoom out, because this isn't really about marketplaces. First you prepare the state, and you keep what the seller wrote apart from facts you trust. Then you ask atomic questions, one idea each. You apply your lines, chosen from what mistakes cost. Anything that's maths or policy stays in code: prices, limits, who's eligible. Whatever the model is unsure about goes to a person. And you log everything, the probabilities, the lines and the outcome, so you can check calibration and drift later.

[click] The same six steps for a bank handling card disputes. To be clear, this one and the next are illustrative designs, not measured results. In the bank, the model never moves money.

[click] Or a factory checking parts from a camera photo. The measurements come from sensors, never from the photo. In every one of them, a probability must never quietly become authority.
